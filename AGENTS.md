# Book editorial project: instructions for Codex

This is a **literary and theological nonfiction manuscript**, not a software project. The current task is a **whole-book structural audit**, not a rewrite.

1. Read `editorial/CODEX_START.md`, `editorial/REVISION_PLAN.md`, and `editorial/chat-context-2026-10-27.md` before analysing prose.
2. The author may supply source files **directly to Codex** as attachments or local workspace files, rather than committing them to GitHub or putting them in a `sources/` directory. Verify the supplied manuscript, Chapters 8–13 V2, Claude's condensed Chapters 8–13, and V2 editorial change record regardless of path. If a required document is genuinely inaccessible, identify it; NEVER substitute older repository PDFs without confirmation.
3. Preserve the thirteen-chapter structure as the initial hypothesis. Do not assume chapters should be shortened, merged or moved.
4. Judge each suspected repeat by its paragraph, chapter, **whole-book progression**, and the reader's experience. A callback that introduces a new dimension or pays off an earlier scene is not redundancy.
5. Always distinguish **the fall being re-established** from **the fall being revisited at a new theological or narrative level**. Keep the latter.
6. No manuscript rewrites, deletions, relocations, merges or silent improvements during phase 1. **Original source files are immutable.** Only write reports under `editorial/reports/`.
7. Every finding must give a locatable source reference: filename, chapter, paragraph ID (or deterministic paragraph number), opening words, and short evidence quotation. No invented lines, citations or invented provenance.
8. For every MOVE/CONDENSE/REMOVE proposal, explain what the original chapter loses, what the destination gains (if any), whether an earlier/later payoff breaks, and how the exact removed material is preserved in a change ledger. A move must make **both** locations better.
9. Preserve individual voice, scene construction, concrete details, nuanced scriptural development, personal testimony, distinctive phrasing and literary motifs. Reject generic “AI cadence,” slogans, template contrasts, therapy register, and unasked-for rewrites.
10. Challenge weak passages with evidence; do not automatically validate the author or indiscriminately cut material. No historical or biblical “corrections” without clear sourcing and a reason they affect the argument.
11. Conduct independent structural, motif/repetition, theological, narrative and preservation reviews, delegating to project subagents **where available**. An integrating editor reconciles disagreements and documents unresolved choices.
12. No publication or uploading of manuscript text to the public GitHub repository during this audit. The repository is public; keep private source materials in the local Codex workspace unless author explicitly approves publication.
13. The author approves every substantive recommendation **before** editing begins. Report disputed decisions as questions, not changes.

**First deliverable:** an evidence-backed editorial map and preservation plan, with the current manuscript unchanged. See `editorial/REVISION_PLAN.md`.
